Double-Digit Fuel Price Hikes Hit Motorists on March 10

DOE warns of staggered increases ranging from ₱17 to ₱24 per liter as Middle East tensions drive global oil benchmarks to historic highs.

By Philippine Star | 03-09-26 | Manila, Philippines

The Department of Energy told motorists to brace for higher fuel prices as local oil retailers implemented double-digit hikes, with increases ranging from ₱17.00 to ₱24.00 per liter. (Philstar.com) DOE Secretary Sharon Garin explained the adjustments would be rolled out on a staggered basis throughout the week, noting the agency's limited authority under the Downstream Oil Industry Deregulation Act. Based on trading data from the Mean of Platts Singapore, kerosene was projected to lead the spike with an unprecedented increase of ₱24.92 per liter, while diesel — the backbone of the nation's transport and logistics sector — was estimated to rise by ₱19.62 per liter, and gasoline by ₱10.43 per liter

The government announced it would provide fuel subsidies for public transport operators, farmers, and fisherfolk, while the Department of Transportation prepared free bus rides to help mitigate the burden of higher prices on ordinary commuters. (GMA News Online) President Marcos also signaled he was considering a temporary reduction or suspension of excise taxes on petroleum products, with the Palace formally set to seek emergency powers from Congress to reduce the fuel tax. The DOE also said it was working with the DILG and the PNP to intensify monitoring against possible hoarding and profiteering in Luzon and Mindanao after receiving reports of potential irregularities.

299 OFWs Repatriated from Middle East as Tensions Escalate

The Department of Migrant Workers facilitates the return of nearly 300 Filipinos from Dubai, with preparations underway for a possible large-scale evacuation.

By [Reporter Name] | [Date] | [Location]

Almost 300 Filipinos repatriated from the Middle East arrived in the Philippines on Thursday, March 5, with 299 individuals landing at Ninoy Aquino International Airport Terminal 3 from Dubai. (Philstar.com) Some returnees were advised to go home by their employers for safety reasons, while others had completed their contracts. The Department of Migrant Workers (DMW) provided them with financial assistance and other forms of government support upon arrival. President Marcos said the government was monitoring conditions of Filipinos in the Middle East through the DFA, DMW, and OWWA, with preparations underway for the possible repatriation of those wishing to return.

OFW remittances from the Middle East represent about 18% of the Philippines' total remittances, with the region accounting for roughly 40% of all overseas Filipino workers. (BusinessWorld) The ongoing conflict has raised fears of a slowdown in remittance flows, which are a vital lifeline for millions of Filipino families. The government stressed there was no mandatory repatriation order yet but remained on standby for a larger-scale evacuation if conditions deteriorate further.

Sara Duterte Impeachment Complaints Pass Substance Test, 54–1

Two active impeachment complaints against the Vice President survive a crucial second preliminary test at the House, clearing the path for a full trial.

By Philippine STAR | 03/09/26 | Manila, Philippines

The House justice committee voted 54–1 to declare the two active impeachment complaints against Vice President Sara Duterte sufficient in substance, with Quezon City 4th District Representative Bong Suntay as the sole dissenting vote. (RAPPLER) The complaints allege the Vice President's misuse of confidential funds, bribery of education officials, unexplained wealth, and alleged threats against the President's life and family. The Vice President has issued blanket denials but has not responded in detail to each message.

The impeachment drive was renewed just three days after the Supreme Court's January 29 ruling affirming that the original articles of impeachment were unconstitutional, with fresh complaints filed on February 2. (GMA News Online) Duterte had earlier been impeached by the House in February 2025, but that process was struck down before a Senate trial could be completed. (Wikipedia) With two complaints now deemed sufficient in both form and substance, the constitutional process is set to roll forward.
